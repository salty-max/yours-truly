# Hemet Nesingwary

**Who.** Hemet Nesingwary (715), a dwarf, "renowned war hero of the Alliance and master big game hunter", at his expedition's camp. The anchor is Big Game Hunter (208): King Bangalash, the white tiger he came to Stranglethorn for. It requires the three masteries' last beasts: Sin'Dall (188, Ajeck Rouack's tigers), Bhag'thera (193, Sir S. J. Erlgadin's panthers), Tethis (197, his own raptors). In the game he talks like a hearty old gentleman ("Jolly nice", "old chap", "Bloody well done! Right-o!", "Cheers to you, I say! Cheers!") and his book, The Green Hills of Stranglethorn, is being proofread by Barnil Stonepot. The side quest of the book's scattered pages (338) isn't required, so the letter never mentions it.

**The voice.** He booms, in the game and on paper: the exclamations are his, where he would shout across the camp. Generous with praise and with himself, easily carried away telling a story, especially his part in it. The camp can interrupt him. Ordinary sentences between the shouts, so the shouts still land.

**Never.** A modern word, cruelty past the hunt, the scattered pages.

**French.** Vouvoie, "$gMon cher:Ma chère;" in the greeting only.

**The game's.** Bangalash and the earlier hunts (208), Ajeck and Erlgadin, Barnil proofreading Hemet's work, the book as a fact of the camp (338), "Bloody well done", "Right-o", "old chap" and the toast (his own lines, 194, 196, 208), his sights on Bangalash "for quite some time" (208). The scattered-page errand is not mentioned. **Ours.** The argument at supper, the kettle, Barnil's question and Hemet mending his own account (his draft, not the book in the game). Nothing changes the readable book already in the game or promises the player a manuscript to collect.

## Letter

My dear $N!

Bangalash! The whole camp is still shouting about him over supper! Sir Erlgadin swears his panther was the harder quarry, and Ajeck won't hear a word of it. I have moved the kettle twice to save it from their demonstrations.

I had my sights on that white devil for a very long time, old chap, and I have earned a word or two on the subject.

Barnil read my account of the hunt and asked where you came into it. A fair question! I'm afraid I got quite carried away with my own attempts and forgot you entirely. Right-o, I have put you back where you belong.

Bloody well done! Cheers to you, I say! Cheers!

Hemet Nesingwary
