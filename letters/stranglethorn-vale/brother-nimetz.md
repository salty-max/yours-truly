# Brother Nimetz

**Who.** Brother Nimetz (739), a priest at the Rebel Camp in northern Stranglethorn Vale, a man. The chain (203, 204, 574, 202, 207, 205, 206): the rebels' war against Colonel Kurzen, "a brilliant tactician" who outnumbers them ten to one (203 to 202, Sergeant Yohwa and Lieutenant Doren); Nimetz: "Colonel Kurzen was once a great, noble man - I must find what caused his fall into depravity! He may be under the sway of troll magic" (207), the four tablets' legends, the Skullsplitter fetishes (205); Kurzen killed in the Stockpile, "We may all breathe easier now that he is gone" (202). The anchor, Mai'Zoth (206): "Kurzen may be dead, but...that which drove him to evil still lives!... An Ogre Mage - Mai'Zoth... He is rumored to employ mind-controlling magic, and I am positive that he corrupted Kurzen. If Mai'Zoth is allowed to live, then his evil may influence others, and may even cause another Kurzen to surface." The Mind's Eye: "You are a great force for good, $N, and may your strength never fade! I will study this Mind's Eye and determine if it may be used for good. If not... then it must be destroyed."

**The voice.** An earnest priest and a scholar of evil: careful, prayerful, plain about temptation. Kind about Kurzen as he was.

**Never.** What the Mind's Eye turns out to be (his study isn't done), Kurzen or Mai'Zoth gone for good (both come back), the rebels' war won, the Mind's Eye anywhere the player could see it.

**French.** Vouvoie.

**The game's.** Kurzen once a great, noble man, his fall, the legends and fetishes, Mai'Zoth and his mind-controlling magic, the Mind's Eye studied for good or destroyed, another Kurzen, may your strength never fade (207 to 206). **Ours.** Not looking into it, first, wrapped and put away, praying and reading beside it, what broke Kurzen came from outside him, a comfort and a warning.

## Letter

$N,

You brought the Mind's Eye out of Mai'Zoth's hands, and the first thing I decided was not to look into it. A man who sets out to learn whether a thing can be used for good should not begin by letting it use him.

It is wrapped and put away. I pray beside it, and I read what others have written of such things. If there is any good in it, I will find it. If there is not, it will be destroyed, and I will not grieve.

I think often of Colonel Kurzen as he was, a great and noble man. The legends, the fetishes, all of it pointed to one truth: what broke him came from outside him. That is a comfort, and also a warning. It could have been any of us.

May your strength never fade, $N.

Brother Nimetz
Rebel Camp
