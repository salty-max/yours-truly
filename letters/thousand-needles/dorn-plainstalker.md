# Dorn Plainstalker

**Who.** Dorn Plainstalker (2986), a tauren at Splithoof Crag in Thousand Needles, who tests the facets of a being. The chain (1149 to 1394): "Many facets make up a being: the spirit, the body, the mind... The first is a Test of Faith. If you have faith, leap from the planks overlooking Thousand Needles" (1149), "You took the leap of faith even though you had no guarantees that you would survive"; the Test of Endurance, Grenka Bloodscreech (1150); the Test of Strength, Rok'Alim (1151); the Test of Lore, "not mine to give": Braug Dimspirit near Talondeep Path ("There is hope you will answer my question correctly then... unless you've forgotten to study its contents", 1154) and Parqual Fintallas in the Undercity ("You're probably a farce; some adventurer that overheard talk in a tavern", 1159). The anchor (1394), Parqual: "Stay true to the balance you've shown throughout these trials, $N. Dorn will test you again in the future if you do"; Dorn: "Ah, $N. It has been a long time since you have last stepped into my home. It seems like many moons have passed since you took your first leap that set you upon your path. And now you return to me stronger than you once were... more enlightened. This pleases me." The leap of faith, years later.

**The voice.** A teacher of balance: calm, unhurried, a little cryptic, the tauren plains in his words. Speaks of facets and paths. Fond, never effusive. Terse by card: under 100 words, and the letter reads complete.

**Never.** A new test (he says he will test again: the letter doesn't), the player pushed or told to come back, Mull's spirits or Hawkwind's family (other tauren, other voices), a judgment of the player's class or strength.

**French.** Vouvoie. His French name carries his title ("Traqueur des plaines Dorn"): he signs Dorn.

**The game's.** The facets, spirit, body and mind, the leap from the planks, Grenka, Rok'Alim, Braug and his book, Parqual who called the player a farce, the balance, many moons since the first leap (1149 to 1394). **Ours.** Another young one who could not leap and was not pushed, faith that is pushed not faith, what each teacher thought, balance rarer than strength.

## Letter

$N,

Another young one stood on the planks above the canyon and could not make the leap. I did not push. Faith that is pushed is not faith.

I told the young one what I told you: the spirit, the body, the mind. You were tested in all three, and none of us made it easy. Parqual thought you were a fraud. Braug thought you might forget your reading. I only ever thought you would leap.

You came to my home, and you came back changed. You came back balanced, and that is rarer than strength.

Keep the balance.

Dorn
