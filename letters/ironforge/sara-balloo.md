# Sara Balloo

**Who.** Sara Balloo (2695), a soldier's widow in Ironforge. Her husband Sully died at the Thandol Span. The player found his last letter in the water (637: "You say you saw my Sully pinned beneath the icy waters?"), carried her note to King Magni (683), who answered by commissioning a memorial (686), cut from Alterac Granite the player fetched from Darrow Hill (689) by Grand Mason Marblesten (700, the anchor). Sully's letter is in the game, a dwarven rewriting of Sullivan Ballou's of 1861: their sons, "my little Edgar", and its last page, "if there be a soft breeze upon your cheeks it shall be my breath". Sara's own note to the King is in the game too: formal, piercing, three questions he can't answer.

**The voice.** Hers is already written: the old dwarven "'Tis", a formal hand, questions that cut, grief held so tightly it shows only at the edges. She asked her king what the war would cost, and his answer is a stone: she says so once, and no more. She writes carefully, sometimes formally, but ordinary words are enough. Her boys ask for the letter to be read again. She need not explain its meaning to the player or end with a line that tells them what to feel. A big letter, up to 180 words.

**Never.** Self-pity, melodrama, cursing the Alliance (she questions, she doesn't renounce), a word against Sully.

**French.** Vouvoie, "$gCher:Chère;" in the greeting only.

**The game's.** The completed memorial, Marblesten and the granite (686 to 700, "Perfect! Better than I could have imagined!"), her question to the King (her note, item 4514: "at what cost?"), the recovered letter (637, item 4432), Sully's sons and Edgar (4432), her formal, exact manner. **Ours.** The King's answer read as granite, the question about the depth of the inscription, the readings with the boys, Edgar learning the pauses, Sara wishing she could answer. No site is invented for the memorial and no reply or keepsake is enclosed.

## Letter

Dear $N,

The memorial is finished, I am told, and Grand Mason Marblesten is pleased with the Alterac granite you brought him. I asked our King what the war would cost us. He has answered me in granite. I asked the mason only whether Sully's name is cut deep enough to stay legible when the stone begins to wear. Perhaps that is a foolish question. It mattered to me.

I have put his letter away several times. Then one of the boys asks what his father said, and I take it out again. Little Edgar knows where I pause now, and waits for me there before I turn the page.

There are things in it Sully never said to me when he was home. 'Tis strange to hear them now, in his own hand. I should have liked to answer him.

Thank you for bringing it back to us. I could not have borne to lose that too.

With gratitude,
Sara Balloo
