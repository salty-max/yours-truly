# "Auntie" Bernice Stonefield

**Who.** "Auntie" Bernice Stonefield (246), "an old widow" of the Stonefield Farm. She lost her necklace and blamed "that guttersnipe Billy Maclure" (247) (85). Billy, too hungry to remember, wanted one of her Pork Belly Pies, so the player brought her boar meat and she baked it, grudging: "you tell him I hope he chokes on it!" (86, 84). Billy, between mouthfuls ("This pie is the best!"), remembered a big gold-toothed kobold carrying the necklace into the Fargodeep Mine (87). With it back, she gave the player her husband's Lion-stamped Gloves (1359), the quest's one reward: "It was my husband's and he always said it was lucky. If only he didn't forget it on his last campaign! *sniff*" He left them at home, and didn't come back.

**The voice.** A farm widow with a sharp tongue and a soft spot she'd rather you didn't notice. Plain country speech, "dear", a grudge against the Maclures kept like the good china. Her grief gets a few plain sentences, then she's back to scolding before it can settle. Signs the way everyone calls her.

**Never.** Grief past a line, the feud ended, Maybell and Tommy Joe (another chain), Goldtooth gone for good (he comes back), a guess whether the player wears the gloves, a name for her husband (the game gives none).

**French.** Tutoie, "$gmon petit:ma petite;": an old countrywoman to a youngster.

**The game's.** The necklace, Billy and her suspicion of him (borne out by his slip: "I think I dropped, er...I mean I saw, the old lady's necklace"), the pie made for the thief and her wish he'd choke on it, Billy calling it the best pie, the kobold and the mine (85 to 87), the gloves, her husband's luck and his last campaign, "warm an old widow's heart" (85, 87). **Ours.** Sleeping in the necklace, Billy telling everyone about the pie, the hook by the door, the gloves going out the door, the P.S.

## Letter

Dear $N,

The necklace is back where it belongs. I sleep in it. Let the kobolds try.

That Billy Maclure tells anyone who'll stand still that my pie was the best he ever ate. Of course it was. That's not the point. The point is he took my necklace and got pie for his trouble, and now half the forest knows my pie better than my name.

I gave you my husband's gloves because they were doing nobody any good on the hook by the door. He always said they were lucky, and then he went off on his last campaign without them. I've looked at that hook a long while. Better they go out the door with somebody.

Thank you, dear. You did warm an old widow's heart, though I'll deny it to any Maclure who asks.

Auntie Bernice

P.S. I didn't put anything in Billy's pie. I thought about it.
