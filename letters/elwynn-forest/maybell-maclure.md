# Maybell Maclure

**Who.** Maybell Maclure (251), a young girl of the Maclure Vineyards, in love with Tommy Joe Stonefield (252), whose family is her family's bitter enemy: "Oh, I'm cursed!" Her love letter went through the player's hands to Tommy Joe at the river (106). Tommy Joe sent the player to his Gramma Stonefield (248) (111), Gramma to William Pestle (253) at the Lion's Pride Inn in Goldshire (107), who brewed an Invisibility Liquor from Crystal Kelp the murlocs had (112), "so she can slip away from the Maclure Vineyards and go to Tommy Joe", lasting "long enough for her to visit". The anchor (114): she takes it, "I feel guilty deceiving my family, but my feelings for Tommy Joe are too strong to ignore... I'll drink this liquor as soon as I have the chance and sneak away to my love." A visit, not an elopement: the game keeps her at the vineyards and Tommy Joe at his river, so her letter tells the visit and the run home.

**The voice.** Breathless and dramatic, as in the game ("What did he say??"): exclamations, double question marks, her heart on her sleeve. Young, and knows it, and doesn't mind. Guilty about her family and too happy to be very sorry. Signs with an initial, in case.

**Never.** An elopement, a wedding or the feud ended (the game keeps them apart), a word against her family, Billy Maclure or the necklace (another chain), an errand (she can't thank Pestle or Gramma herself, and never asks, not even as a question, that the player do it).

**French.** Vouvoie, as in the game: a well-brought-up girl confessing everything to a near stranger.

**The game's.** The feud, the love letter carried (106), Gramma Stonefield and William Pestle (111, 107), the liquor and its purpose, a visit long enough to see him, her guilt at deceiving her family (112, 114), Tommy Joe at the river (106). **Ours.** How the visit went (her footsteps, his jump, the laughing, her hands coming back), the run home, the thanks she can't give, the letter in return, the initial.

## Letter

$N!

I did it!! I drank every drop and walked right out past the house, and nobody saw a thing. Not one thing! I could hear my own feet, and I was sure the whole vineyard could too.

Tommy Joe was at the river, just where he always is. Of course he couldn't see me either, so I had to say his name, and he jumped so high I thought he'd go in. Then he laughed, and then I did, and we talked until I could see my own hands again. I ran all the way home.

I know it was wicked to deceive my family. I would do it again in a heartbeat!

I can't even thank Mr Pestle or Gramma Stonefield, because I'm not supposed to know either of them!

You carried my letter to Tommy Joe. This one is for you.

All my love (but not that kind!),
M.
