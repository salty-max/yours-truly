# Chief Hawkwind

**Who.** Chief Hawkwind (2981), chief of Camp Narache, where every tauren starts. The anchor is Journey into Thunder Bluff (775): the Rites of the Earthmother done, and the young tauren standing before Cairne Bloodhoof on the highest mesa. Before it: the humble task (752, 753, his mother Greatmother Hawkwind gone to the well, her water pitcher carried back), the Rite of Strength (757, Bristleback belts), Baine Bloodhoof, who knew the chief's carvings on the totem he sent (763), the Rite of Vision (767 to 772, the Water of the Seers), the Rite of Wisdom (773, the Ancestral Spirit at Red Rocks). A first letter, around level 10: the one that shows a new player what the addon is.

**The voice.** An elder chief, slow and warm, fond of his mother and a little afraid of her. His mother has opinions of her own, and the tribe has ordinary news. His pride in the reader comes through in whom he tells. His hands know carving better than writing (Baine knows him by his carvings). He does not need to turn the pitcher into a lesson.

**Never.** Grandeur, Cairne's words put in his mouth, a lesson spelled out, Thunder Bluff named in French (Forever's French name for area 1638 is unsettled).

**French.** Tutoie.

**The game's.** His mother, her trip to the well and the carried pitcher (752, 753), Baine's friendship with Hawkwind and the chief's carvings (763), the Rites and the meeting with Cairne (775). **Ours.** His mother's questions about food, her exchange with her son, Baine's message and its reading at the fire, her verdict on the letter. No new feat or off-screen decision is attributed to the player.

## Letter

$N,

My mother was glad to hear that you had stood before Cairne, young one, and her first thought was whether you had eaten before the climb. I could not tell her, and she feels a chief should know such things.

She still walks to the well for her own water. When I offered to carry the pitcher, she asked if a chief had nothing better to do. When I said that you had carried it for her once, she told me I could have fetched the water in the time it took me to say so.

Baine sent word of you, and I read it to the others by the fire. The little ones made me read it twice, and they have talked of the Rites every evening since.

My mother says this letter has too many corners, like my carvings. I told her you would read it anyway.

Walk with the Earthmother,
Chief Hawkwind
